# 5 GDPR gaps most small businesses don't know they have

**Subtitle:** And the simple fixes for each one, from ten years of running privacy in house.

---

Welcome to The Privacy Blueprint.

Most small businesses aren't ignoring data protection. They simply don't know where the gaps are until a customer, a client or the ICO asks a question they can't answer.

After ten years of running a privacy function inside a large UK organisation, I've noticed the same five gaps come up again and again. None of them are complicated to fix. Most take an afternoon.

Here they are, with what to do about each one.

## 1. A privacy policy that doesn't match what you actually do

**What I see:** A policy copied from a free generator or a competitor's website. It lists tools the business doesn't use, misses the ones it does, and says nothing about how long data is kept.

**Why it matters:** Your privacy policy is a legal promise. If it doesn't describe what really happens to people's data, it isn't doing its job, and it's often the first thing a client or the ICO will read.

**The fix:**
- List every tool that touches customer data (email marketing, payments, booking, forms, your CRM)
- Check your policy names each one and explains why you use it
- Add how long you keep each type of data
- Put a reminder in your diary to review it every 12 months

## 2. A cookie banner without a real "reject" option

**What I see:** A banner with a big "Accept" button and no way to say no, or a "Reject" option buried two clicks deep. Sometimes analytics and advertising cookies load before anyone has clicked anything.

**Why it matters:** Non-essential cookies, such as advertising pixels, need consent before they're set, and saying no must be as easy as saying yes. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 relaxes the rules for some low risk analytics cookies, but advertising and tracking cookies still need consent.

**The fix:**
- Make "Reject" as visible and easy as "Accept"
- Check that nothing non-essential loads until someone agrees
- Ask yourself whether you need every tracking tool you're running. Fewer tools means less to manage

## 3. No data processing agreements with your suppliers

**What I see:** Businesses using Mailchimp, Stripe, Google Workspace, Calendly and a dozen other tools, with no record of the data protection terms in place with any of them.

**Why it matters:** When a supplier handles personal data for you, UK GDPR requires a written contract covering how they protect it. If one of them has a breach, you'll need to show you checked.

**The fix:** The good news is that most large providers already include a data processing agreement (DPA) in their terms, and some ask you to accept it in your account settings.
- Make a simple list of your suppliers
- Find each one's DPA (search "[tool name] DPA")
- Note where it is and the date you checked

That list alone puts you ahead of most small businesses.

## 4. Not paying the ICO data protection fee

**What I see:** Business owners who've never heard of it, or who assume it only applies to large companies.

**Why it matters:** Most organisations that handle personal data must pay an annual fee to the ICO unless they're exempt. Non-payment can lead to a fine, and the register is public, so anyone can check whether you're on it.

**The fix:** Use the ICO's short online self assessment to see whether you need to pay. For most micro businesses the fee starts at £52 a year (£47 by direct debit). It takes about 15 minutes.

## 5. No plan for when someone asks for their data

**What I see:** No process at all, until an email arrives saying "please send me all the data you hold about me".

**Why it matters:** That's a subject access request. You normally have one month to respond, and it's usually free of charge for the person asking. Under pressure, with a deadline running, it's easy to miss something or share too much.

**The fix:** Write a one page process now, before you need it:
1. Who receives and logs the request, and the date it arrived
2. How you confirm the person is who they say they are
3. Where to search (inbox, CRM, booking system, payment records, shared drives)
4. Who checks the response before it goes out
5. A calendar reminder for the deadline

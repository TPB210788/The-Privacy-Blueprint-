# The 7 data protection questions investors will ask you in due diligence

**Subtitle:** How to have the answers ready before they ask, and why it can speed up your round.

---

You've had the pitch meetings. An investor is interested. Then the due diligence questionnaire arrives, and somewhere between the cap table and the IP assignments is a section on data protection.

For a lot of founders, this is where things slow down. Not because they've done anything wrong, but because nobody has ever written the answers down.

Investors ask about data protection for a simple reason: personal data is a liability if it's handled badly. A breach, a regulator complaint or a customer contract you can't fulfil all affect what the business is worth. A clear, documented position tells them you're a lower risk investment.

Here are the seven questions that come up most often, what investors are really asking, and what a good answer looks like.

## 1. "What personal data do you hold, and where is it?"

**What they're really asking:** Do you understand your own business?

**What good looks like:** A simple data map or Record of Processing Activities (ROPA). It lists each type of personal data (customers, users, staff, marketing contacts), why you hold it, which systems it sits in, and who it's shared with.

**Quick win:** Start with a spreadsheet. One row per type of data, with columns for purpose, system, lawful basis, retention period and suppliers involved. An afternoon's work, and it answers half the questions below.

## 2. "What's your lawful basis for processing it?"

**What they're really asking:** Is your business model built on data you're allowed to use?

**What good looks like:** A clear lawful basis for each activity. For example, contract for delivering your service, consent for marketing emails, legitimate interests for basic product analytics (with a documented assessment to back it up).

**Watch out for:** Marketing lists built from contacts who never opted in, and using customer data for a new purpose, such as training an AI model, without checking you're allowed to.

## 3. "Are your privacy notice and cookie set up compliant?"

**What they're really asking:** Would a quick look at your website raise red flags?

**What good looks like:** A privacy notice that matches what you actually do, and a cookie banner where rejecting is as easy as accepting, with nothing non-essential loading before someone agrees.

**Quick win:** Read your own privacy notice side by side with your list of tools. Anything missing or out of date is an easy fix.

## 4. "Who are your key suppliers, and do you have data processing agreements in place?"

**What they're really asking:** If a supplier gets it wrong, are you covered?

**What good looks like:** A list of the suppliers that handle personal data for you (hosting, email, payments, CRM, analytics, AI tools), with a data processing agreement in place for each. Where data goes outside the UK, a note of the safeguard used, such as the UK International Data Transfer Agreement, the UK Addendum to the EU clauses, or the UK-US data bridge.

**Quick win:** Most large providers include a DPA in their terms. Find each one, save a copy, and record the date you checked.

## 5. "Have you had any data breaches, complaints or contact from the ICO?"

**What they're really asking:** Is there anything we should know about?

**What good looks like:** An honest answer, backed by a breach log. If the answer is "none", a log that shows you'd have recorded one is still reassuring. If something did happen, show what you learned and changed.

**Quick win:** Create a breach log now, even if it's empty, and write a one page plan for the first 72 hours after a breach.

## 6. "How do you handle requests from people about their data?"

**What they're really asking:** Can you meet your legal deadlines as you scale?

**What good looks like:** A short written process for subject access, deletion and correction requests. Who handles them, how identity is checked, where you search, and how you meet the one month deadline.

**Quick win:** Write the process on one page and test it on yourself. Ask your own systems for "everything you hold about me" and see how long it takes.

## 7. "Are you using AI or other high risk processing, and have you assessed it?"

**What they're really asking:** Is there a hidden regulatory risk in your product?

**What good looks like:** Where you're doing anything high risk, such as using AI on personal data, large scale monitoring or processing sensitive data, a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) that shows you identified the risks and reduced them.

**Watch out for:** Personal data being pasted into AI tools without anyone checking the provider's terms. It's one of the most common gaps in fast growing teams right now.

## Your investor ready checklist

Before your next round, aim to have these in one folder:

- [ ] Data map or ROPA
- [ ] Lawful basis for each processing activity
- [ ] Up to date privacy notice and cookie set up
- [ ] Supplier list with data processing agreements
- [ ] International transfer safeguards noted
- [ ] Breach log and breach response plan
- [ ] Data subject request process
- [ ] DPIAs for any high risk processing
- [ ] ICO registration confirmed

A tidy folder like this won't just get you through due diligence. It can shorten it, and it tells investors you run the business properly.

# Substack engagement plan

15 minutes, 2 or 3 times a week. Comment on 2 posts, share 1 Note.

**Golden rules**
- Add something useful in every comment. Never "Great post!"
- No links in comments. People click your profile if the comment is good
- Check the post is recent (last 2 to 4 weeks) before commenting
- Subscribe (free) to each newsletter so new posts land in your inbox

## Ready to post Notes (Substack's feed)

Post 2 or 3 a week. Copy and paste as they are.

1. Most small business privacy policies were copied from a generator and never touched again. Quick test: does yours name every tool that handles customer data? If not, it isn't doing its job.

2. A cookie banner with a big "Accept" button and a hidden "Reject" isn't compliant. Saying no should be as easy as saying yes.

3. Using Mailchimp, Stripe or Google Workspace? Each needs a data processing agreement. The good news: most include one in their terms. Find it, save it, note the date. That's it.

4. The ICO data protection fee starts at £52 a year for most micro businesses, and the register is public. Worth 15 minutes to check you're on it.

5. Someone emails asking for "all the data you hold about me". You normally have one month. Write your one page process now, not when the email lands.

6. Raising investment? The data protection section of due diligence is where rounds quietly slow down. A data map, supplier list and breach log in one folder makes you look like a business that runs properly.

7. Investors don't expect a startup to have perfect data protection. They want to see you know what personal data you hold, why, and who you share it with.

8. Before pasting customer data into an AI tool, check one setting: does the provider train on your data? Turning it off takes minutes.

9. A breach log you've never had to use is still worth having. It shows you'd have noticed.

10. Data protection isn't about having every policy. It's about knowing what you hold, why you hold it, and what you'd do if it went wrong.
